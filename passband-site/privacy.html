Privacy Policy

Effective August 25, 2026

Passband is an email client for Mac and iOS. Behind it runs squelchd, a daemon that syncs your Gmail mailbox, triages it, and lets you act on it. This policy explains what data the software touches, where that data lives, and what reaches infrastructure we operate.

Two tiers, two very different answers

Passband is offered in two forms, and the honest answer to "where does my mail live" is completely different in each. Read the one you use.

Self-host

You run squelchd on hardware you control. Your mail, your search index, and your Google OAuth tokens are stored on your machine and never transit or rest on our infrastructure. We could not read your mail if we wanted to; we hold no copy and no key. The only things that ever reach us are the optional relay services described below, which carry timing and opaque identifiers and never message content.

Hosted (Passband accounts at signup.passband.app)

We run squelchd for you, one isolated instance per account, on infrastructure we operate. Your mail content and your Google refresh token are stored on our servers. Holding them is what the hosted tier is; always-on triage and mobile push are not possible otherwise. The rest of this policy sets out exactly what we hold, how it is protected, who can reach it, and how to get rid of it.

Google user data

Passband accesses Gmail through Google APIs with your consent, using OAuth. It requests exactly these scopes and nothing else:

ScopeWhat it is used for
gmail.readonly Sync message headers and bodies so the daemon can triage them, render threads, search them, and extract deadlines, shipment tracking, and calendar details.
gmail.modify Apply the outcome of a triage or an explicit user action: archive, label, mark read or unread, park a thread for later. Never used by the sync path.
gmail.send Send mail you compose in Passband: replies, forwards, new messages, and invitations you choose to share from your own address.
userinfo.email, openid Confirm which mailbox a token belongs to, and identify your account at sign-in. The daemon refuses to store a credential whose mailbox does not match the configured account.

Read access and write access are held in separate credential slots, and the sync pipeline is structurally unable to load the write slot. On self-host, write access is a second, separate consent you can decline and never grant. On hosted, all three scopes are granted in a single consent at signup, because a hosted account has no second path back to Google; a partial consent provisions nothing at all.

Every write action is user-initiated or the direct result of a rule you configured, and is recorded in an audit log on your own daemon.

Limited Use disclosure

Passband's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In particular:

  • Google user data is used only to provide and improve the user-facing features of Passband: triaging, displaying, searching, and acting on your mail at your direction.
  • It is never sold, never used for advertising or ad targeting, and never transferred to third parties except as explicitly described in this policy.
  • No human reads it except with your explicit consent, for a support request you have made, or where required for security investigation or legal compliance.
  • Google user data is never used to develop, improve, or train generalized or non-personalized artificial intelligence or machine learning models. Where message content is sent to a model provider (see below), it is sent for inference on your own mail only, under terms that prohibit training on it.

Where your data is stored

Self-host

Message content and metadata are stored in a local SQLite database on your machine. OAuth tokens are stored in your operating system keychain (macOS) or a credentials file with restrictive permissions in a location you designate (Linux). Neither ever transits or rests on our infrastructure.

Hosted

Each hosted account gets its own isolated daemon instance and its own SQLite database on a dedicated volume, on a server we operate at Hetzner Cloud (Germany). Specifically, we hold:

  • Your synced mail. Message headers, bodies, attachments, labels, and derived triage data, in your account's own database.
  • Your Google refresh token, encrypted to a per-account key that exists only as an encrypted-at-rest secret in our cluster. Our signup and control-plane services do not retain the plaintext token after provisioning.
  • Your account record: the email address you signed up with, your account label, timestamps, and the invite you used. This is stored in a managed PostgreSQL database at Railway.
  • Encrypted backups. Your database is continuously replicated to Cloudflare R2 object storage, encrypted before it leaves our server with a key held outside that storage. Server disk snapshots are retained by Hetzner for 7 days.

We do not have per-account access controls that would prevent our own operators from reaching a hosted account's data: as with any hosted service, whoever administers the servers can in principle read what is on them. Access is limited to Passband operators, is used only for the reasons listed in the Limited Use disclosure above, and if you would rather not extend that trust to anyone, the self-host tier exists exactly for you and is free.

Automated processing by language models

Passband uses large language models to score importance, summarize threads, extract deadlines and shipment details, and power the built-in assistant. Where the request goes depends on your tier, and this is the most consequential difference between them.

Self-host: your own key, or nothing

These features are off until you supply your own Anthropic or OpenAI API key. With a key configured, the daemon sends message excerpts directly to that provider under your own account and their terms. With no key, triage runs on local heuristics only and no message content leaves your machine.

Hosted: our gateway, our provider account

Hosted accounts do not bring a key. Your daemon sends message content to an LLM gateway we operate at bifrost.passband.app, which forwards it to Anthropic under a Passband account, using a per-account credential with a spending limit. Message content therefore passes through our infrastructure and reaches Anthropic as a subprocessor. Anthropic does not train models on this content. The gateway records per-account token counts and spend so budgets can be enforced; message content is not used for any purpose other than serving your request.

What is never sent to a model, on either tier

Security-sensitive mail (verification codes, one-time passcodes, password resets, and similar) is detected deterministically, before any model is consulted, and is never sent to a model, never exposed to the assistant, and never crosses the agent-facing interface.

Services we operate

ServiceWhat it sees
Push relay Device tokens, opaque event identifiers, and optional collapse identifiers, forwarded to Apple Push Notification service. It retains nothing and cannot see message content, senders, or subjects.
Read receipts (opt-in) If you enable open tracking on mail you send, the relay briefly buffers an opaque token, a timestamp, and a user agent string until your daemon collects them. Only your daemon can map a token back to a message. Collected entries are deleted from the relay.
LLM gateway (hosted only) Message content in transit to Anthropic, plus per-account token and spend accounting. Request and response bodies are not retained.
Signup and control plane (hosted only) Your email address, account label, invite code, and provisioning state. Also the waitlist form: an email address and whatever you type into it.

Relay logs contain request timing and status codes only. Device tokens, open tokens, and payloads are never logged.

Third parties you direct us to, on either tier

  • Shipment carriers. If you configure your own carrier API credentials (UPS, FedEx, DHL, USPS), your daemon sends tracking numbers it found in your mail directly to that carrier to fetch delivery status. We never proxy a carrier call. With no credentials configured, no carrier is ever contacted.
  • Your own model provider. On self-host, the API key you supply determines who receives message excerpts, under your account and their terms. We are not a party to that flow.
  • People you write to. Mail you send, forward, or share an invitation to goes from your own Gmail address to the recipient you chose. Invitation recipients are never disclosed to us.

App telemetry

The Passband apps report anonymous usage events (for example, "an email was archived") to PostHog to help us understand which features are used. Event names and property values come from a fixed allowlist enforced in code: message content, subjects, senders, recipients, labels, and search queries are structurally unable to leave the app through this channel. Events are keyed to a random per-install identifier, or for hosted accounts to an opaque account identifier that is not your email address. You can reduce or disable telemetry in Settings under Privacy.

Retention and deletion

Self-host: your data is retained on your machine for as long as you keep it. To delete everything, delete the local database and credentials (or keychain entries) and revoke Passband's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions. We hold no copy, so there is nothing for us to delete.

Hosted: your mail is retained for as long as your account exists. To delete it, email us at the address below and we will destroy the account's database, volume, encryption key, and stored refresh token. Once the per-account key is destroyed the encrypted backups are permanently unreadable, and they age out of object storage and out of Hetzner's 7-day snapshots after that. Revoking Passband at myaccount.google.com/permissions immediately ends our access to your mailbox on either tier, and is effective whether or not you have asked us to delete anything.

Security

The read credential and the write credential are held in separate slots, and the sync pipeline is structurally unable to load the write slot. Agent-facing interfaces are read-only and never expose security-sensitive mail. Self-hosted daemons bind to loopback by default. Hosted accounts are isolated from one another at the container, network, and storage layers, and each has its own encryption identity. Secrets are encrypted at rest. The full threat model is public at SECURITY.md.

To report a vulnerability, email the address below. We will acknowledge within 72 hours.

Children

Passband is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect data from them.
