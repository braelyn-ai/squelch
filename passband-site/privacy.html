Privacy Policy

Effective August 7, 2026

Passband is a Mac email client, and squelchd is the self-hosted daemon behind it. You run both on hardware you control. This policy explains what data the software touches, where that data lives, and what, if anything, ever reaches infrastructure we operate.

The short version: your mail is synced, analyzed, and stored on your own machine. We do not run servers that receive, store, or can access your email or your Google credentials.

Google user data

Passband accesses Gmail through Google APIs, with your consent, using OAuth. It requests:

  • Read access (gmail.readonly) to sync your messages so the daemon can triage them.
  • Optional write access (gmail.modify, gmail.send), granted separately and only if you enable it, so you can archive, label, and send mail from Passband. Every write action is user-initiated, requires explicit confirmation, and is recorded in a local audit log.

Message content and metadata are stored in a local SQLite database on your machine. OAuth tokens are stored in your operating system keychain or a local credentials file you designate. Neither ever transits or rests on our infrastructure.

Limited Use disclosure

Passband's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In particular:

  • Google user data is used only to provide and improve the user-facing features of Passband: triaging, displaying, and acting on your mail at your direction.
  • It is never sold, never used for advertising, and never transferred to third parties except as described below at your direction.
  • No human reads it except with your explicit consent, or where required for security or legal compliance.
  • Google user data is never used to develop, improve, or train generalized or non-personalized artificial intelligence or machine learning models.

Optional AI processing, with your own keys

Two features can send message content to a large language model provider, and both are off until you supply your own API key:

  • LLM triage. If you configure an Anthropic or OpenAI API key, the daemon sends message excerpts to that provider, under your own account and their terms, to score importance and extract deadlines and summaries. Without a key, triage runs on local heuristics only. Security-sensitive mail (verification codes, password resets, and similar) is detected deterministically before any model is consulted and is never sent to a model.
  • The assistant. Passband's built-in assistant uses your own Anthropic API key. Content it reads on your behalf goes to Anthropic under your account.

In both cases you choose the provider, you hold the key, and you can remove it at any time. We are not a party to that data flow.

Infrastructure we do operate

Two small services run on our infrastructure. Both are designed to see timing and opaque identifiers, never content:

  • Push relay. To deliver notifications, your daemon sends the relay device tokens, opaque event identifiers, and optional collapse identifiers. The relay forwards to Apple and retains nothing. It cannot see message content, senders, or subjects.
  • Read receipts (opt-in). If you enable open tracking on mail you send, the relay briefly buffers an opaque token, a timestamp, and a user agent string until your daemon collects them. Only your daemon can map a token back to a message. Collected entries are deleted from the relay.

Relay logs contain request timing and status codes only. Device tokens, open tokens, and payloads are never logged.

App telemetry

The Passband app reports anonymous usage events (for example, "an email was archived") to help us understand which features are used. Event names and values come from a fixed allowlist enforced in code; message content, subjects, senders, labels, and search queries are structurally unable to leave the app through this channel. You can reduce or disable telemetry in Settings under Privacy.

Retention and deletion

Your data is retained on your machine for as long as you keep it. To delete everything: delete the local database and credentials files (or keychain entries), and revoke Passband's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Because we hold no copy of your mail or tokens, there is nothing for us to delete on our side; relay buffers are transient and deleted on collection.

Security

The read credential and the write credential are held in separate slots, and the sync pipeline is structurally unable to load the write slot. Agent-facing interfaces are read-only and never expose security-sensitive mail. The daemon binds to loopback by default. The full threat model is documented in SECURITY.md.

Children

Passband is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect data from them.
